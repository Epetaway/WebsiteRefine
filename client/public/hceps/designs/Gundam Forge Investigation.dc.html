HAVING ACCESS TO INFORMATION ≠ BEING ABLE TO MAKE SENSE OF IT.
01 HUMAN CONTEXT

Information about a trading card game is fragmented by nature.

Card text, cost, effects, restrictions, synergies with other cards, deck legality — all of it is technically available somewhere, usually spread across a rulebook, a card database, and whatever a player can hold in their head.

Most deck-building tools respond by adding more panels, more stats, more visible data. Gundam Forge tests a narrower premise instead — more information does not automatically create better decisions.

The interface should surface relationships and constraints — not make the strategic choice for the player.

THE PREMISE, NARROWED

Not more panels — fewer, better-placed facts.

Constraints appear at the moment they're relevant.

The system never says which card to play.

02 DESIGN DECISIONS

Three decisions carried the most weight.

Each moves information to the moment of decision — and stops short of making the decision itself.

Decision support is less about adding information than deciding what should become visible at the moment a decision is being made.

D·01 Validation inside the workflow The deck-builder recomputes legality on every edit and shows it in a persistent status bar — there is no separate "check my deck" step, and never a blocked edit.
D·02 Surface, don't decide Constraints and relationships are shown at the moment they're relevant. The system never tells a player which card to play — or which three cards would complete the deck.
D·03 Local-first, grown carefully The architecture grew from client-only toward Supabase-backed services as real requirements (auth, cross-device sync) appeared — while the deck builder itself stayed fully local-only, by design, the whole way through.

04 REFLECTION

Decision support is less about adding information than deciding what should become visible at the moment a decision is being made.

Whether the validation I built actually reduces errors without also reducing experimentation — and how a system like this should communicate uncertainty rather than false confidence — remains open.

PRELIMINARY — Gundam Forge is a working beta built and tested by one person. Its decision-support claims are design hypotheses — they have not been tested against real players making real deck-building decisions.

The Gundam Card Game is the intellectual property of Bandai Namco. Gundam Forge is an independent, non-commercial fan and educational project, not an official product. No Gundam artwork or card imagery appears in this portfolio.
