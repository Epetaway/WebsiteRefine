ABOUT

Code, culture, and discipline.

I began in graphic design, where I learned to organize information and communicate visually. Front-end engineering taught me how those ideas become working systems. Accessibility taught me that every technical decision shapes who can participate. Teaching Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu showed me how people learn — through feedback, repetition, trust, and progressive challenge. Human-computer interaction is where those experiences meet.

Designer turned engineer, always a student.

I graduated from Kean University with a BFA in Graphic Design, then built on that foundation with an IBM Full Stack Software Developer Professional Certificate. That combination shapes how I work — I think about layout, hierarchy, and visual weight the same way I think about component structure and state management.

The career has spanned media marketing platforms, financial services, nonprofit campaigns, and healthcare portals — but the discipline is the same everywhere: understand who's using the thing before deciding how it should work.

Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu trained me in a different kind of precision — discipline, patience, refinement under pressure. Teaching it showed me how people actually learn, which is the same lens I bring to interface design.
