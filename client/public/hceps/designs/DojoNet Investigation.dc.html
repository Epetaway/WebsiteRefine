Can technology help people see their training without pretending it can replace the teacher?

A martial arts training and social platform — a feed for training partners, a profile system, and a Training Journal that tracks technique proficiency without flattening it into a checklist or a percentage bar.

01 HUMAN CONTEXT

The shape of learning doesn't compress into one number.

Progression in martial arts is not linear, not binary, and not a checklist. Someone can perform a technique correctly in drilling and still fail to recognize the same opportunity live. Someone can plateau for weeks and then have something click all at once.

A single progress bar or completion percentage misrepresents that — not because the data is wrong, but because the shape of learning doesn't compress into one number.

I came to this as both the confused student and the instructor explaining the same idea six different ways.

WHY A BAR LIES

Correct in drilling ≠ recognized live.

Weeks of plateau, then it clicks all at once.

Techniques don't finish. "Done" is the wrong claim.

02 DESIGN DECISIONS

Three decisions that came directly out of building it.

Each one is a refusal — of a percentage, of default cloud sync, of automated advice. The system's restraint is the design.

Even the four-stage model is a simplification I'm not fully at peace with — an approximation, not a solved representation of mastery.

D·01 Qualitative stages Proficiency is tracked in four stages — learning, practicing, comfortable, mastered — per technique, not a single percentage.
D·02 Data stays local Journal entries, technique notes, and sparring logs live with the practitioner by default — not synced to any server, not visible to anyone else. Right now, no sharing mechanism exists at all.
D·03 Description, not prescription The system surfaces training hours, session vitals, and per-session-type breakdowns. It does not interpret them or say what to do next — that's a coaching judgment it doesn't attempt, and currently has no interface for a coach to make either.

04 REFLECTION

Representing progress can change how people understand progress — measurement itself is a design decision, not a neutral act of showing data.

DojoNet is closer to its product direction than its final implementation — and it is, honestly, an active design investigation rather than a finished product.

PRELIMINARY — The training-journal features described here are real and working; the coaching loop the project is ultimately about is not built yet. Student and instructor comprehension, learning outcomes, and long-term motivation effects are all unvalidated.

OPEN QUESTIONS

What kinds of progress can software represent responsibly?
